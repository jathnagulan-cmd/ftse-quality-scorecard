EquiSift

Quality UK companies at sensible prices, sifted from the FTSE 100

Every company is scored against its peers on four things read from its annual reports: how profitable it is, how strong the balance sheet is, whether profit turns into cash, and what the market charges for it. Banks, insurers and property companies are left out because these measures don't fit them.

A screen for deciding what to research, not a list of what to buy. Scores use annual reports only. News, results since the annual report and new chief executives or finance directors show as Check first warnings but don't change the scores, and Yahoo's data has gaps. Read the latest results before acting. Not financial advice.

How this was tested, and why it's weak evidence

At each date below, every company was rescored using only the accounts published by then and that day's share price, then followed for twelve months, dividends included. "Top" and "bottom" are the best and worst fifth of companies by overall score. The periods overlap and all sit in one market, the universe is today's FTSE 100 (so companies that dropped out are missing), and trading costs are ignored. Treat it as a sign the quality scores aren't noise, not as proof.

The long-term list needs three years of margins, so it only exists from 2025. Returns are total returns over twelve months unless marked, each list held in equal amounts.

Scores are relative: each metric is ranked against the other companies (margins and debt against industry peers), then averaged, so 100 means best in the group. Overall weights the three quality pillars and valuation equally.

Data from Yahoo Finance; free data has gaps and errors. Code, methods and known limitations: github.com/jathnagulan-cmd/ftse-quality-scorecard
